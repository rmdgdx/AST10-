1.DNA replicationApply

After one round of semiconservative replication, what should each daughter DNA molecule contain?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Each parental strand templates a complementary new strand. Replication does not preserve the entire original duplex in only one daughter, and its product is DNA rather than an mRNA hybrid.

2.Transcription and directionApply

A DNA template is written 3′–TAC CTT AAA–5′. Which RNA is transcribed from it?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

RNA is synthesized 5′→3′ and is complementary to the template: T pairs with A, A with U, C with G, and G with C. Option B uses DNA bases; C reverses the product's direction labels.

Review the lecture section →
3.TranslationApply

Using AUG = methionine, GAA = glutamate, UUU = phenylalanine, and UGA = stop, what product follows from 5′–AUG GAA UUU UGA–3′?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The three sense codons specify three amino acids. UGA terminates translation and does not encode a stop amino acid. Producing complementary RNA would not be translation.

Review the lecture section →
4.Gene regulationAnalyze

A nutrient increases a gene's mRNA abundance, but sequencing detects no DNA change. Which explanation should be investigated first?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

RNA abundance can change through its production or degradation without a DNA-sequence change. The observation alone supports neither speciation nor a changed genetic code.

Review the lecture section →
5.Mendelian inheritanceApply

For Aa × Aa with complete dominance and equal segregation, what is the probability that one offspring has genotype aa?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Each parent contributes a with probability 1/2, giving 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4. This is a probability for each offspring, not a guaranteed count in every four offspring.

Review the lecture section →
6.LinkageAnalyze

Two loci close together on one chromosome produce fewer recombinant combinations than expected under independent assortment. Which interpretation is best?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Nearby loci tend to be transmitted together. Crossing over can generate recombinants, but physical linkage changes the expected frequencies. Dominance concerns phenotype expression, not chromosome assortment.

Review the lecture section →
7.Genotype and environmentAnalyze

Plants of the same genotype grow to different heights under different nutrient supplies. Which conclusion is justified?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Phenotype reflects genotype and environment. A difference in growth does not by itself demonstrate mutation or a different genotype, and dominance does not imply a particular height or advantage.

Review the lecture section →
8.Synonymous mutationApply

A coding change alters an mRNA codon from GAA to GAG; both encode glutamate. How should the change be described?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The nucleotide sequence changed but the encoded amino acid did not. Synonymous does not guarantee complete biological neutrality, because RNA processing, stability, or translation can sometimes be affected.

Review the lecture section →
9.FrameshiftAnalyze

Which change most directly shifts the downstream reading frame in the middle of a protein-coding region?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

An insertion or deletion not divisible by three changes triplet grouping. Removing three bases preserves the downstream frame, although it can still harm the protein.

Review the lecture section →
10.Horizontal gene transferApply

A bacterium acquires a resistance plasmid through direct contact with another cell. Which process best fits the observation?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Direct-contact DNA transfer is conjugation. Binary fission increases cell number, translation makes protein, and drift is a population-level sampling process. Transformation would involve environmental DNA and transduction a viral vector.

Review the lecture section →
11.Natural selectionAnalyze

A heritable resistance variant is detected before treatment and becomes more frequent after resistant cells leave more descendants. What best explains the increase?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Variation existed and resistant cells contributed more descendants under the treatment. This is selection; organisms do not intentionally generate the variant they need. New mutation or transfer could also occur, but is not required by this scenario.

Review the lecture section →
12.Selection calculationApply

A culture starts with 900 susceptible and 100 resistant cells. Treatment leaves 90 susceptible and 80 resistant survivors. What is the resistant frequency among survivors?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The denominator is the surviving population: 80/(90 + 80) = 80/170 ≈ 0.471. The initial fraction was 10%; resistant frequency can rise even when the absolute number of resistant cells falls.

Review the lecture section →
13.Founder effectAnalyze

Five randomly sampled colonists establish an isolated population. Its allele frequencies differ from the source, without evidence of allele-associated reproductive advantage. What mechanism best fits?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A small founding sample may carry unrepresentative allele frequencies by chance. Isolation alone does not establish selection, and the described population is not receiving continuing gene flow.

Review the lecture section →
14.Hardy–Weinberg reasoningApply

At a diploid two-allele locus satisfying Hardy–Weinberg assumptions, p = 0.6 and q = 0.4. What heterozygote frequency is expected?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Heterozygotes have frequency 2pq = 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.48. The values 0.36 and 0.16 are the expected homozygote frequencies, p² and q².

Review the lecture section →
15.Reproductive barriersApply

Two populations inhabit the same region but breed in different seasons and rarely mate. Which barrier is illustrated?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Different breeding seasons reduce mating before fertilization, making this a prezygotic temporal barrier. Hybrid sterility and inviability act after fertilization.

Review the lecture section →
16.Microbial species conceptsEvaluate

Why is the biological species concept based on interbreeding insufficient for many bacterial lineages?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The interbreeding criterion was developed for sexual populations and cannot simply be applied to asexual lineages. Bacterial delimitation uses multiple lines of evidence; shape alone is insufficient.

Review the lecture section →
17.Binomial nomenclatureApply

In the scientific name Halobacterium salinarum, which interpretation follows binomial nomenclature?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

A binomial contains a capitalized genus and lowercase specific epithet, both italicized. The epithet alone is incomplete. Halobacterium is an archaeal genus despite the wording of its name.

Review the lecture section →
18.Dichotomous keysApply

In the lecture's teaching key, a sample has a demonstrated nucleus, no chloroplasts, budding cells, and a chitin-containing wall. Which route and identification fit?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A nucleus takes the sample to step 2. The combination of no chloroplasts, budding, and a chitin-containing wall selects 2b. The key is restricted to its defined groups and does not identify every unknown microorganism.

Review the lecture section →
19.Reading a cladogramAnalyze

In the rooted topology (O,(A,(B,(C,D)))), which statement is correct?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

C and D form a sister pair. Rotation around their shared node does not alter the topology. Living tips are not automatically ancestors or ranked by evolutionary advancement.

20.Conflicting charactersAnalyze

In the supplied matrix, characters 1, 2, 3, and 6 support nested groups, but character 4 occurs only in A and D. What is the most defensible response?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The conflicting pattern may require independent gains or gains and losses on the inferred tree. It should be evaluated explicitly rather than forcing a relationship from one similarity. Parsimony compares change counts under stated assumptions.

Review the lecture section →
21.Gene treesEvaluate

Two well-studied microbial genes support different trees, and one gene has evidence of horizontal transfer. What is a reasonable interpretation?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Horizontal transfer can move a gene between lineages, causing its tree to differ from the broader lineage history. Multiple loci and explicit analysis can help; one discrepancy does not erase shared ancestry.

Review the lecture section →
22.Domains of lifeApply

A confirmed prokaryotic isolate lacks peptidoglycan and has characteristic ether-linked isoprenoid membrane lipids. Which domain is most strongly supported?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

This combination is characteristic of Archaea. Microscopic size does not identify a domain. Viruses are acellular and are not a fourth domain of cellular life.

Review the lecture section →
23.Viruses and cellsAnalyze

A sample contains particles that replicate only using a host cell and lack cellular organization. Why should they not simply be assigned to a fourth domain of cellular life?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Viruses are studied in microbiology but do not have the cellular organization used to define Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. Microscopic size and host association alone do not create a cellular domain.

Review the lecture section →
24.Microbial metabolismApply

A microbe oxidizes hydrogen for energy and builds biomass carbon mainly from CO₂. What nutritional description best fits?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Chemical reactions provide energy, inorganic hydrogen is an electron donor, and CO₂ supplies carbon. Those three axes identify chemolithoautotrophy; they do not remove the need for suitable reaction partners and nutrients.

Review the lecture section →
25.Microbial growthApply

Starting from 100 cells, a culture undergoes three ideal doublings with no cell loss. How many cells are expected?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

N = N₀ × 2ⁿ = 100 × 2³ = 800. Doubling is multiplicative rather than an addition of 100 cells per generation. The ideal model does not imply indefinite growth in a closed culture.

Review the lecture section →
26.Detection versus activityEvaluate

DNA is detected in a cold environmental sample, but no growth or metabolic activity has been measured. Which conclusion is warranted?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

DNA can persist independently of current growth, and contamination must also be assessed. Genetic detection, viability, metabolism, and reproduction answer different questions.

Review the lecture section →
27.Cold adaptationAnalyze

A cold-adapted microbe maintains membrane function at temperatures that reduce membrane fluidity in a comparison strain. Which mechanism is most relevant?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Lipid composition can help preserve membrane function at low temperature. This is a physiological mechanism, whereas a change of label or removal of the membrane does not explain the observation.

Review the lecture section →
28.Salt stressAnalyze

A salt-tolerant cell accumulates compatible solutes when external salinity rises. What problem does this response primarily address?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Compatible solutes help balance osmotic conditions without unduly disrupting cellular components. They are not proof that the organism can grow under every salt composition, temperature, or water activity.

Review the lecture section →
29.Survival versus growthEvaluate

Cells remain viable after a brief radiation exposure but reproduce only after transfer to favorable conditions. What does this experiment directly support?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Recovery establishes that at least some cells survived. It does not demonstrate reproduction during exposure, radiation-powered metabolism, or the suitability of a different planetary environment.

Review the lecture section →
30.Habitability inferenceEvaluate

One Earth microbe tolerates high salinity and another grows in cold conditions. What is the strongest next step when evaluating a dark, cold extraterrestrial brine?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Separate tolerances do not demonstrate that one organism can grow under the combined conditions. Habitability is conditional and differs from detecting life; chemical energy requires suitable reactions, not merely a possible donor.

Task 1Build and evaluate a cladogram · 10 points

Five hypothetical taxa were scored for six independent binary characters. O is the designated outgroup. State 0 is absence and 1 is presence of the specified derived feature; no cells are missing data. Treat characters as equally weighted and gains and losses as equally costly.

Character matrix: six independently observed structural or sequence features, C1–C6

  1. Draw a rooted tree with O outside A–D; map the minimum number of changes for each character.
  2. Compare your tree with (O,((A,D),(B,C))). Report both total scores.
  3. Identify shared derived characters, the D-only character, and the conflicting character. Explain why a conflict does not justify ignoring the other evidence.
  4. State one limitation of parsimony and one reason a microbial gene tree might differ from a lineage tree.
Model answer and rubric

A best-supported topology is (O,(A,(B,(C,D)))). C1 supports A–D; C2 and C6 support B–D; C3 supports C–D. C5 is unique to D and does not resolve relationships among the other tips. Minimum changes for C1–C6 are 1, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1: total 7. C4 can be mapped as separate gains in A and D; this homoplasy conflicts with the nested pattern.

The alternative has counts 1, 2, 2, 1, 1, 2, totaling 9. It fits C4 with one change but worsens C2, C3, and C6. Equal-weight parsimony favors the seven-change tree among these alternatives. Convergence, unequal evolutionary rates, or horizontal gene transfer can complicate inference. Counting fewer changes does not prove historical truth.

Rubric: topology and labels 2; character mapping 3; scores and comparison 2; conflict interpretation 2; limitation 1.

Task 3Evaluate a cold-brine habitability claim · 10 points

A hypothetical dark subsurface brine has temperature −5°C, pH 7, and 15% dissolved salts by mass. Hydrogen is detected; water activity, electron acceptors, nutrient availability, and long-term stability have not been established. These values are classroom assumptions, not measurements from a real planet.

Earth analogueEvidence under separate laboratory conditionsMechanism
HGrowth at 20°C and 15% salt; cold growth untested.Compatible-solute accumulation.
CGrowth at −5°C and 1% salt; 15% salt untested.Cold-active enzymes and membrane-lipid adjustment.
RSurvives a radiation pulse; reproduces only after transfer to favorable medium.DNA repair and protection of cellular components.
  1. Write a 300–500-word case distinguishing what is measured, inferred, and unknown.
  2. Explain why combining H's salt limit with C's cold limit does not prove that either grows in the brine.
  3. Propose a controlled conceptual experiment testing temperature and salt jointly; include favorable-condition controls, sterile blanks, replicates, and measurements of cell increase or biomass through time.
  4. Identify at least three additional environmental measurements and explain why each matters. Conclude with a conditional habitability claim, not a claim of detected life. Cite two credible sources.
Model reasoning and rubric

The evidence supports H's growth in one warm saline condition, C's growth in one cold dilute condition, and R's survival of a specific exposure. It does not establish combined-stress growth. A factorial comparison could test each analogue at −5°C and 20°C crossed with 1% and 15% salts of specified composition. Compare repeated biomass or cell-count measurements, activity indicators, and recovery tests with controls; distinguish growth from persistence of DNA or initial cells.

Measure water activity to assess accessible water; electron acceptors and reactant concentrations to evaluate energy-yielding reactions; nutrient supply to assess biosynthesis; and persistence of favorable conditions. Hydrogen alone does not show usable energy. A defensible conclusion is: the brine is a candidate habitat if combined conditions permit sustained metabolism and reproduction; the supplied data establish neither that capacity nor actual inhabitants.

Rubric: claim–evidence distinction 2; mechanism–stress reasoning 2; controlled test 3; missing measurements and limitations 2; sources 1. This is a paper analysis, not an instruction to culture environmental samples.
