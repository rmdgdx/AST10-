IInquiry, the characteristics of life, and levels of organisation Items 1–8

Lecture reference: §1.1–§1.3.

1.FalsifiabilityEvaluate

Four students propose explanations for why a hot-spring microbe is found only above 70 °C. Which statement is a scientific hypothesis in the strict sense?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Only B forbids an observation: if cultures grow well at 50 °C, B is refuted. A and C restate the observation in vague terms and forbid nothing; D is not testable at all.

2.Experimental designAnalyze

A researcher grows seedlings under red, blue, and white light, watering the red-light group more because it looked dry. Blue-light seedlings grow tallest. The most serious flaw is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

A controlled variable was allowed to vary between groups, so any difference could be due to water rather than light. White light was in fact included (it serves as a reference). Three treatment levels are fine, and height is a standard measure.

3.Control groupsAnalyze

In the 90 °C growth test from the lecture, a sterile tube of medium is incubated alongside the inoculated tubes. Its function is to

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The sterile control isolates the effect of the inoculum: if it stays clear while inoculated tubes cloud, the turbidity is attributable to the organism. Medium does not 'grow', and a sterile tube adds no replicate of the treatment.

4.Correlation and causationEvaluate

Across 40 lakes, phosphorus concentration correlates strongly with algal biomass (r = 0.85). Which additional evidence would most strengthen the claim that phosphorus causes algal growth?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Only manipulation with a matched control establishes causation. More correlational data (A) cannot exclude confounders; C adds another correlation; D is authority, not evidence. This is the classic Lake 226 experiment.

5.Theory versus hypothesisEvaluate

A commentator writes that 'evolution is only a theory, so it is no more certain than any guess.' The best scientific response is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The commentator uses the everyday meaning of 'theory'. Scientific theories (cell theory, germ theory, evolution) are the most robust category of explanation, not the least. Theories are not promoted to laws; laws describe, theories explain.

6.Applying the characteristics of lifeEvaluate

A robotic probe returns images of spherical objects on an icy surface that grow by accretion, split when large, and appear to move toward warmer regions. Which single further observation would most change the assessment from 'physical process' toward 'possibly biological'?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Growth, division, and movement toward gradients all have physical mimics (crystals, bubbles, thermophoresis). Heritable variation with fitness consequences is the one characteristic with no abiotic mimic. Carbon, size, and water raise plausibility but do not discriminate.

7.Emergent propertiesAnalyze

Which is the best example of an emergent property?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Emergence is a property of arrangement that the parts lack. Composition (A, D) and simple additivity (C) are not emergence.

8.Levels of organisationAnalyze

A biologist studies how nutrient runoff from farms changes the abundance of fish, algae, and bacteria in a bay, and how oxygen in the water responds. The level of organisation studied is the

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The study includes several interacting populations (community) plus abiotic components (nutrients, oxygen), which together define the ecosystem level.

IIAtoms, bonds, and water Items 9–16

Lecture reference: §1.4–§1.5.

9.Why carbonEvaluate

Silicon is more abundant than carbon in Earth's crust and, like carbon, forms four covalent bonds. The strongest chemical reason terrestrial life is carbon-based rather than silicon-based is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Carbon's advantage is bond chemistry: strong, comparable C–C, C–H, and C–O bonds allow chains, rings, and multiple bonds in water. Silicon's chemistry in an aqueous, oxygen-rich setting collapses to silicates; its CO₂ analogue is a solid.

10.Bond typesAnalyze

Two DNA strands are held together by A–T and G–C pairing. That the strands can be separated by gentle heating (~90 °C) but the sugar-phosphate backbone survives indicates that base pairing uses

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Individually weak hydrogen bonds (2–3 per pair) break with modest heat while the covalent backbone (hundreds of kJ/mol per bond) does not. This difference is what makes DNA both stable and readable.

11.Polarity and solubilityAnalyze

Molecule X consists almost entirely of C–H bonds; molecule Y has several –OH and –NH₂ groups. Predict their behaviour in water.

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

C–H bonds are nonpolar; –OH and –NH₂ are polar and hydrogen-bond with water. This is the same rule that drives phospholipid bilayer formation and protein folding.

12.Ionic bonds in waterAnalyze

A salt crystal is hard and requires high temperature to melt, yet dissolves within seconds in water. The best explanation is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Ionic bonds are strong in the dry lattice but weak in water because hydration shells shield the charges. No covalent bonds are involved and no reaction occurs; the ions are simply separated.

13.Specific heatEvaluate

Two hypothetical planets receive identical sunlight. One is covered by a global ocean of water, the other by an ocean of a liquid with one-quarter of water's specific heat. Which prediction follows?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Specific heat governs how much temperature changes per unit of energy absorbed or lost. Lower specific heat means larger swings for the same energy flux. Nothing about mean temperature or absorption follows from specific heat alone.

14.Ice densityEvaluate

If water contracted on freezing like most substances, the most important biological consequence for a temperate lake would be that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Ice floats because hydrogen bonding forms an open lattice ~9% less dense than liquid. Denser ice would sink, exposing new surface water to freezing and progressively solidifying the lake.

15.pH calculationAnalyze

Gastric fluid is about pH 2; blood is about pH 7.4. The hydrogen ion concentration in gastric fluid exceeds that in blood by a factor of roughly

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

pH is –log₁₀[H⁺], so the difference of 5.4 units is 10⁵·⁴ ≈ 250,000-fold. The temperature caveat (D) affects the neutral point of water slightly, not the definition of pH or this ratio.

16.Buffers and CO₂Evaluate

A patient hyperventilates, rapidly blowing off CO₂. Using the carbonic acid–bicarbonate equilibrium, predict the immediate change in blood pH and its direction.

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. Removing CO₂ shifts the reaction leftward, combining H⁺ with bicarbonate and raising pH (respiratory alkalosis). Buffers resist but do not eliminate the change.

IIIMacromolecules, energy, and enzymes Items 17–23

Lecture reference: §1.6–§1.7.

17.Origin-of-life chemistryEvaluate

Origin-of-life researchers regard the formation of the first polymers as harder to explain than the formation of monomers principally because

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Le Chatelier's principle: in water, a reaction that produces water is pushed toward reactants. Cells solve this with enzymes and ATP; prebiotic scenarios invoke wet–dry cycling, mineral surfaces, or vents. Monomers, by contrast, form readily (Miller–Urey, meteorites).

18.Membrane self-assemblyEvaluate

Phospholipids placed in water spontaneously form bilayers and vesicles. Why is this observation important for hypotheses about the origin of life?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Compartmentalisation is one of the three hard problems (with polymerisation and heredity). Self-assembly demonstrates a route that needs no prior biology. It does not prove what the first cells were (A), and lipids carry no sequence information (D).

19.Protein structureAnalyze

An enzyme from a hot-spring archaeon works at 95 °C, while the human version of the same enzyme denatures at 55 °C. Both have similar primary sequences. The most likely basis of the difference is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Thermostability comes from the number and arrangement of weak interactions that maintain tertiary structure, not from the covalent backbone. Sequence can be similar while a few substitutions add salt bridges or tighten the hydrophobic core.

20.Chirality as a biosignatureEvaluate

A returned sample contains amino acids with an 80:20 excess of the L form over the D form. Compared with a sample containing only abiotic-looking organics, this finding is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Enantiomeric excess is a recognised biosignature class, but 80:20 is suggestive rather than conclusive: some abiotic processes produce modest excesses, contamination must be excluded, and an independent origin might have chosen D. 'Suggestive' is the calibrated word.

21.Second law and lifeEvaluate

A student claims that because cells become more ordered as they grow, life violates the second law of thermodynamics. The correct rebuttal is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The second law applies to the total system. Local order is bought with a larger disorder exported to the surroundings. This is why life needs a continuous energy flux and why it is a disequilibrium state.

22.Enzymes and equilibriumAnalyze

Adding an enzyme to a reaction mixture at equilibrium would be expected to

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Enzymes lower activation energy in both directions equally; they do not change ΔG or the equilibrium constant. At equilibrium there is no net reaction to accelerate.

23.Energy gradients and habitabilityEvaluate

Cassini detected molecular hydrogen in the Enceladus plume, and the ocean is thought to contain oxidants such as sulfate and carbon dioxide. In habitability terms, this matters because

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Life needs an exploitable free-energy gradient, not sunlight specifically. Terrestrial methanogens live on exactly this H₂ + CO₂ couple. Hydrogen alone is not a biosignature; in fact its abundance suggests it is not being consumed rapidly.

IVHomeostasis, ecology, and the astrobiology thread Items 24–30

Lecture reference: §1.8–§1.10.

24.Classifying feedbackAnalyze

During a fever, a rising body temperature does not trigger sweating until the temperature exceeds about 39 °C, after which sweating brings it back to 39 °C. The best description is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The loop still opposes deviation, but around a new, higher set point established by pyrogens acting on the hypothalamus. A failed loop would allow unlimited drift; positive feedback would not stabilise at any value.

25.Diagnosing control failureEvaluate

Three habitats each show a CO₂ control problem. Habitat A: CO₂ drifts steadily upward with no correction. Habitat B: CO₂ swings between 0.2% and 1.2% every two hours. Habitat C: CO₂ is stable but at 1.5%. Match each to the most likely fault.

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Drift indicates no correction (detection or actuation lost). Oscillation indicates over-correction or lag. Stable-but-wrong indicates the loop works but defends the wrong value. This is the same diagnostic logic as in physiology.

26.Positive feedbackAnalyze

Which is an example of positive feedback, and why is it not destabilising for the organism?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Positive feedback amplifies until a terminating event. Clotting, labour, and the action potential are self-limiting by design. The other three are negative feedback loops.

27.Trophic efficiencyAnalyze

A sealed habitat's crop chamber fixes 5,000 kJ of energy per day. If the crew ate only fish raised on those crops, the energy available to the crew would be about

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Two transfers at ~10%: crops → fish (500 kJ) → crew (50 kJ). Eating the crops directly makes ~500 kJ available. This is why bioregenerative designs minimise trophic levels.

28.Nitrogen cycleEvaluate

A habitat design supplies crops with fixed nitrogen only from stored fertiliser, with no recycling of crew waste and no nitrogen-fixing organisms. The critical long-term flaw is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Matter cycles; it is not created. Without a closed nitrogen loop (waste processing returning ammonium/nitrate, or fixation from the N₂ reservoir), the system runs down. This is an ecological constraint, not an engineering preference.

29.Habitable versus inhabitedEvaluate

A planetary scientist describes a subsurface Martian aquifer as 'habitable'. Which claim is the scientist entitled to make on that basis?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Habitability is an environmental assessment against stated criteria. Inhabitation requires detection. Conflating the two is the most common error in public discussion of the field.

30.Universal requirement versus terrestrial factEvaluate

In writing an operational definition of life for a mission, which item should be treated as a requirement any life must meet, rather than a feature of Earth life that may be contingent?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Boundary, disequilibrium, and energy flux follow from thermodynamics and apply to any chemistry. DNA, the specific amino acid set, and ATP are implementations that another origin could have solved differently. A definition built on B is more general and more measurable.

VPortfolio and applied tasks Graded outputs

These produce the syllabus output for Week 1: Portfolio Entry 1: Life and biological organization map. Evaluation: Analytic rubric; answer key; feedback notes.

Task 1Life and biological organisation map (Portfolio Entry 1)Create

Produce a one-page map with three layers: (1) the levels of organisation from atom to biosphere with one emergent property at each level; (2) the characteristics of life with a non-living counterexample for each and a mark on those you would require before reporting a detection; (3) an operational definition of life in under 100 words, followed by one sentence naming the kind of life your definition would fail to detect.

Instructor note and grading focus

Grade for accuracy of levels and properties (40%), quality of counterexamples (20%), measurability of the definition (30%), and the honesty of the limitation statement (10%). A definition that omits evolution is acceptable if the limitation sentence acknowledges what is lost.

Task 2Biomolecule and homeostasis analysisCreate

Choose one homeostatic variable (temperature, glucose, pH, or osmolarity). Draw the loop with receptor, control centre, set point, and effector labelled. Identify the macromolecules involved at each step (for example, the enzyme or hormone that acts as the effector signal) and state which macromolecule class each belongs to. Then predict the observable consequence of (a) halving the receptor's sensitivity and (b) doubling the loop delay.

Instructor note and grading focus

The predictions are the graded element: (a) should describe a widened tolerance band or slower correction, (b) overshoot and oscillation. Credit correct macromolecule classes for hormones (protein or steroid) and enzymes (protein).

Task 3Concept check: solvent alternativesCreate

In 200 words, evaluate whether the argument 'liquid water is required for life' is a physical requirement or an Earth-based assumption. Use at least three properties of water from §1.5 and state, for each, whether an alternative solvent could supply it.

Instructor note and grading focus

Strong answers separate properties that follow from polarity (solvent action for ions, hydrogen bonding) from those that are quantitative advantages (specific heat) and name a specific alternative (ammonia, methane) with its temperature range.
