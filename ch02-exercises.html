ICell theory, size, and cell architectures Items 1–8

Lecture reference: §2.1–§2.3.

1.Resolution versus magnificationAnalyze

A student photographs a bacterium at 1,000× with a light microscope, then enlarges the image 10× on screen. The internal ribosomes (about 20 nm) are still invisible. The reason is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Resolution is limited by wavelength; digital enlargement magnifies the blur. Electron microscopy is needed for 20 nm objects.

2.Surface-area-to-volumeAnalyze

Cell P is a 2 μm cube; cell Q is a 20 μm cube. Compared with Q, P has a surface-area-to-volume ratio that is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

For a cube, SA/V = 6/s. P: 3 μm⁻¹; Q: 0.3 μm⁻¹. A tenfold size difference gives a tenfold ratio difference in favour of the smaller cell.

3.Size limits and 'nanofossils'Evaluate

Structures 30–100 nm across in a Martian meteorite were once proposed as fossil bacteria. The strongest cell-biological objection is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A lower size limit follows from the volume needed for the minimal molecular machinery of a self-maintaining cell. Objects well below it are more plausibly mineral artefacts.

4.Classifying a cellAnalyze

A cell has a nucleus, 80S cytoplasmic ribosomes, mitochondria, a cellulose wall, and no centrioles. It is best classified as a

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Nucleus and 80S ribosomes make it eukaryotic; a cellulose wall and absence of centrioles are plant features. Animal cells lack walls; prokaryotes lack nuclei.

5.Endosymbiosis evidenceEvaluate

Which observation would most weaken the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The theory predicts mitochondrial genes should resemble a specific bacterial lineage (α-proteobacteria), which they do. Closest similarity to the host nucleus instead would remove the strongest line of evidence. The other three are supporting observations.

6.Archaeal membranesAnalyze

Archaea living at 95 °C and pH 2 often have membrane lipids that are ether-linked and span the membrane as a single layer. Compared with a bacterial bilayer, this design

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Ether bonds resist hydrolysis at low pH; a covalently linked monolayer cannot come apart when heated. Both stabilise the barrier under extreme conditions.

7.Organelle function from structureAnalyze

A cell type packed with rough ER, prominent Golgi, and many secretory vesicles is most likely specialised for

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Rough ER synthesises secreted proteins, the Golgi packages them, and vesicles export them. This is the profile of pancreatic acinar cells and plasma cells.

8.Endomembrane routingAnalyze

A mutation prevents the addition of the mannose-6-phosphate tag in the Golgi. The most direct consequence is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The tag is the sorting address for lysosomal enzymes. Without it they follow the default secretory route. This is the basis of I-cell disease.

IIMembranes and transport Items 9–16

Lecture reference: §2.4–§2.6.

9.Membrane fluidityAnalyze

Salmon living in near-freezing water are found to have membranes rich in unsaturated fatty acids. The functional reason is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Double bonds create kinks that hold neighbouring lipids apart; without them the bilayer would gel and transport would stop.

10.Selective permeabilityAnalyze

Which pair correctly matches a substance with how it crosses the plasma membrane?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Glucose is large and polar and needs a GLUT carrier; O₂ diffuses freely; Na⁺ is charged and needs a channel or pump; steroids are lipid-soluble and diffuse through the bilayer.

11.Tonicity calculationAnalyze

A cell with internal osmolarity 300 mOsm is placed in 150 mM MgCl₂. Predict its behaviour.

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Osmolarity counts particles: MgCl₂ → Mg²⁺ + 2 Cl⁻. Water leaves the cell toward the more concentrated exterior.

12.Plant versus animal in hypotonic mediumAnalyze

Distilled water is fatal to red blood cells but beneficial to wilted lettuce. The difference arises because

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Both cell types take up water in a hypotonic medium. The wall limits plant cell volume and generates turgor; the wall-less animal cell has no such limit and lyses.

13.Diagnosing transport typeEvaluate

Uptake of solute S by a cell is specific, saturates at high concentration, is unaffected by metabolic poisons, and stops when the external concentration equals the internal. S enters by

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Specificity and saturation indicate a protein; independence from ATP and cessation at equilibrium indicate passive movement. Only facilitated diffusion fits all four.

14.Na⁺/K⁺ pump consequencesEvaluate

Ouabain inhibits Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Which set of consequences is correctly predicted for an animal cell?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Loss of the pump collapses the Na⁺ and K⁺ gradients (potential decays), removes the driving force for secondary active transport (uptake slows), and lets intracellular solute and water rise (swelling).

15.Secondary active transportAnalyze

Oral rehydration solution contains glucose and sodium together, not sodium alone, because

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

SGLT1 couples Na⁺ entry (down its gradient) to glucose entry (against its gradient); the solute uptake then draws water osmotically. This is why the therapy works even in cholera, where other mechanisms fail.

16.Gradients as evidence of lifeEvaluate

A sample from a subsurface brine contains cell-like particles. Which measurement would most directly distinguish living cells from dead cells or abiotic vesicles?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Lipids, shape, and size are shared by dead cells and abiotic vesicles. A gradient maintained against leakage, and dependent on metabolism, is the signature of ongoing work.

IIIRespiration and photosynthesis Items 17–23

Lecture reference: §2.7–§2.8.

17.Role of oxygenAnalyze

Oxygen is described as essential to aerobic respiration, yet it participates directly in none of the reactions of glycolysis or the citric acid cycle. Its actual role is to

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Without an acceptor the chain stalls, NADH accumulates, and the NAD⁺ needed upstream runs out. Oxygen's job is to keep electrons flowing.

18.Pasteur effectEvaluate

A yeast culture consumes glucose 12 times faster after oxygen is removed, while its growth rate falls. The best explanation is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Energy demand is met by consuming more substrate through the low-yield route; growth still falls because total ATP supply is lower.

19.UncouplingEvaluate

Dinitrophenol makes the inner mitochondrial membrane leaky to protons. Predicted effects on oxygen consumption and ATP synthesis are

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Protons bypass ATP synthase, so the gradient never builds; the chain runs faster trying to rebuild it (more O₂ used) but no ATP results. The same mechanism, via uncoupling protein, generates heat in brown fat.

20.Fermentation versus anaerobic respirationAnalyze

A subsurface bacterium oxidises hydrogen and reduces sulfate to sulfide, with no oxygen present, and yields several ATP per hydrogen consumed. This metabolism is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Fermentation has no chain and no external acceptor; this organism has both, with sulfate in place of oxygen. Sulfate reducers are major players in sediments and hydrothermal systems.

21.Universality of chemiosmosisEvaluate

Chemiosmosis is found in mitochondria, chloroplasts, and bacterial membranes. The strongest inference from this distribution is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Near-universal distribution plus the endosymbiotic origin of organelles points to deep ancestry. This is why proton gradients are central to hydrothermal-vent origin-of-life models (Week 11).

22.Source of photosynthetic oxygenAnalyze

Algae are supplied with water containing ¹⁸O and CO₂ containing ordinary ¹⁶O. The oxygen gas they release is enriched in ¹⁸O. This demonstrates that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The label appears in O₂ only when it starts in water. Photolysis at photosystem II splits water, not carbon dioxide.

23.Photosynthesis without oxygenEvaluate

An exoplanet shows a strong 'red edge' spectral feature suggestive of pigment-bearing surface life but no detectable atmospheric oxygen. A defensible interpretation is that

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Anoxygenic photosynthesis is older and widespread on Earth; a productive biosphere need not oxygenate its atmosphere. Absence of O₂ is not evidence against photosynthesis.

IVDivision, variation, and the astrobiology thread Items 24–30

Lecture reference: §2.9–§2.10.

24.Chromosome countingAnalyze

A cell with 2n = 8 is in anaphase of mitosis. The number of chromosomes moving toward each pole is

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

In anaphase the sister chromatids separate and each becomes a chromosome; 16 chromatids become 16 chromosomes, 8 to each pole. Each daughter cell ends with 2n = 8.

25.Checkpoint logicEvaluate

A cell line has lost functional p53. Compared with normal cells exposed to the same dose of radiation, these cells are expected to

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

p53 halts the cycle and triggers repair or apoptosis in response to damage. Without it, damaged cells proceed and propagate errors, which is why p53 loss is so common in cancer.

26.Spindle inhibitorsAnalyze

Colchicine binds tubulin and blocks microtubule assembly. Dividing cells treated with it accumulate in

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Without a spindle, kinetochores cannot attach, the spindle checkpoint blocks anaphase, and cells arrest with condensed chromosomes. This is why colchicine is used to prepare karyotypes.

27.Meiosis versus mitosis outcomesAnalyze

Which statement correctly distinguishes the products of the two divisions?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Mitosis copies; meiosis halves chromosome number and generates variation through crossing over and independent assortment.

28.Sources of variationAnalyze

Two siblings with the same parents are genetically different mainly because of

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Each parent produces gametes with ~8.4 million chromosome combinations before crossing over is counted; each fertilisation samples one from each. Somatic mutation and diet do not change the inherited genome.

29.NondisjunctionAnalyze

A gamete with two copies of chromosome 21 fertilises a normal gamete. The zygote will have

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Nondisjunction in one parent produced an n + 1 gamete (24); with a normal gamete (23) the zygote has 47, trisomy 21.

30.Cellular properties and detectabilityEvaluate

Of the four cellular properties collected in §2.10 (boundary, maintained gradients, redox metabolism, replication with variation), which can a remote spectroscopic observation of an exoplanet atmosphere address, and which cannot?

Reveal answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Atmospheric gas mixtures out of equilibrium are the remote trace of metabolism. Cell boundaries, gradients, and heredity require samples. Knowing which property each method reaches is the core of life-detection design (Week 13).

VPortfolio and applied tasks Graded outputs

These produce the syllabus output for Week 2: Portfolio Entry 2: Annotated cell and transport model. Evaluation: Scoring guide; item analysis; corrective feedback.

Task 1Annotated cell and transport model (Portfolio Entry 2)Create

Produce a labelled diagram of one cell type (bacterial, archaeal, plant, or animal) with every structure named and its function stated in one line; five transport events drawn across the membrane, one each of simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis, primary active transport, and either secondary active or bulk transport, each annotated with direction relative to gradient and energy source; and a 150-word note on which features would leave a detectable trace after one billion years in sediment.

Instructor note and grading focus

Rubric: structural accuracy (35%), transport annotations (35%), preservation note (20%), clarity (10%). The preservation note should name lipids and mineralised walls as durable and organelle organisation as not.

Task 2Transport and metabolism simulationCreate

Using any cell-simulation resource or a spreadsheet model, vary external osmolarity from 100 to 900 mOsm for a model cell of 300 mOsm and record predicted volume. Then model ATP yield per glucose under aerobic, fermentative, and sulfate-respiring conditions (use 30, 2, and 4 ATP) and compute the glucose required to supply 10⁹ ATP per second in each case. Present both as graphs with a paragraph of interpretation.

Instructor note and grading focus

Look for correct recognition that volume responds to total particle concentration, and that substrate demand scales inversely with yield. The graph axes and units are part of the grade.

Task 3Cell comparison briefCreate

Write a 250-word brief comparing a bacterial and an archaeal cell for a habitability assessment of a hot, acidic environment. State which structural features favour each in that setting, which would be preserved as biomarkers, and what an instrument would need to measure to tell the two apart.

Instructor note and grading focus

Credit ether versus ester lipids, absence of peptidoglycan, monolayer membranes, and the point that both are prokaryotic in organisation so morphology alone cannot distinguish them.
